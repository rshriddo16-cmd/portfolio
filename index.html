Multimodal Cashless Public Transit

Designed an integrated multimodal cashless transit ecosystem covering Dhaka Metro Rail (MRT Line 6), city buses, Hatirjheel water taxis, CNG auto-rickshaws, and cycle/battery rickshaws. EXPLORE PROJECT (WEB REPORT) ↗
01.1 // THE PROBLEM SYNC → VIEW A: 1.57 PARADOX

Multi-Leg Cash Friction & The 1.57 Satisfaction Paradox

Dhaka commuters rarely complete a journey in a single vehicle—chaining feeder rickshaws, MRT Line 6 or buses, and last-mile CNGs. While MRT-6 uses smart cards, every feeder leg demands exact physical change ("bhangti"), causing 1–5 minute boarding delays, driver MFS cash-out losses (18.5 Tk per 1,000 Tk), and 15–25% owner ticket leakage. In our foundational survey (N = 150: 100 commuters, 50 workers), commuters rated cashless ease at 3.60/5.0 and time savings at 3.55/5.0, yet overall satisfaction collapsed to 1.57/5.0 (Mode = 1.0) because cashless acceptance was trapped on isolated routes.

● Highlights: 1.57 Satisfaction vs. 3.60 Ease Construct Bars
01.2 // MY CONTRIBUTION SYNC → VIEW B: 4-TIER & HUB MATRIX

4-Tier Transit Hierarchy & Econometric Survey Design

I architected a 4-tier modal hierarchy matching payment hardware to boarding latency: Tier 1 (MRT-6) with <1s NFC tap cards, Tier 2 (City Buses & Hatirjheel Water Taxis) with 2–5s handheld validators, Tier 3 (CNGs) with QR + automated daily owner rent ("Jama") splitting, and Tier 4 (Rickshaws) with 0-Tk laminated Bangla QR stickers and button-phone voice alerts. I designed the dual commuter (N ≥ 450 target; N = 87 digital responses logged) and operator (N ≥ 60) survey instruments across Motijheel, Mirpur 10, Agargaon, and Rampura/Hatirjheel.

01.4 // INSIGHT & OUTCOME SYNC → CORRELATION & POLICY VERDICT

Solving Both Commuter Latency & Driver "Jama" Liquidity

Statistical modeling in Orange Mining and Python proved a strong positive correlation between perceived ease and time savings (Pearson r = +0.697), while worker collection ease showed high dispersion (Mean = 2.90, CV = 0.448) driven by cash-out fees and evening "Jama" obligations. Combining sub-second NFC on trunk lines with zero-fee QR and automated owner-driver rent splits resolves both sides of the market.

● Highlights: Policy Impact & Econometric Findings

Developed a 3-page predictive healthcare dashboard in Google Looker Studio correlating meteorological triggers (rainfall, temperature, humidity, and climate lag windows) with infectious disease outbreaks across Bangladesh's 8 divisions, surfacing CFR %, high-risk demographics, and early-warning lead times.

EXPLORE PROJECT (LOOKER STUDIO) ↗
02.1 // THE PROBLEM SYNC → PAGE 1: CLIMATE-OUTBREAK CURVE

Reactive Reporting vs. Climate-Driven Outbreak Lags

Infectious diseases in Bangladesh follow distinct seasonal climate triggers—monsoon stagnation drives vector-borne Dengue and Malaria, flooding accelerates waterborne Cholera and Diarrhea, and winter cold snaps (<20°C) trigger Nipah and Pneumonia spikes. Yet historical health surveillance across DGHS, IEDCR, and WHO records is typically reviewed after hospital surges peak rather than mapped against meteorological lead windows.

● Switches Dashboard to: ClimAlert Executive Command Center

02.4 // INSIGHT & OUTCOME SYNC → VERIFIED LAG & CFR INSIGHTS

6–8 Week Dengue Lead Windows & Winter Mortality Targeting

The model surfaces an actionable 6–8 week early-warning lead window for Dengue after monthly rainfall exceeds 150 mm at 25–35°C (Rainfall r = +0.82, Temp r = +0.65, with 55%+ cases in Dhaka Division and peak incidence in ages 15–44). Conversely, winter drops below 20°C immediately flag high-CFR Nipah (r = -0.78, NW belt Rajshahi/Rangpur) and pediatric/geriatric Pneumonia (r = -0.72, CFR 1.57%) within a 1–3 week window.

● Highlights: Dengue (r = +0.82) vs. Winter Nipah/Pneumonia Lags

03.1 // THE PROBLEM SYNC → VIEW 3: MARKET & TRUST CRISIS

Fragmented Group Travel, Syndicate Pricing & Western AI Bias

Domestic travelers account for 97.7% of Bangladesh's tourism activity (~BDT 910.7B), and roughly 70% of domestic trips are taken in student, friend, or family groups. Yet travelers rely on unverified Facebook pages, face peak-season syndicate price hikes, and coordinate budgets manually. When we initially tested generic LLM assumptions, the AI defaulted to Western solo travelers with credit cards—missing Bangladesh's group-travel and mobile-wallet (bKash/Nagad) reality.

● Switches Workbench to: Bangladesh Market & AI Pivot Analysis

Bilingual A-to-Z Itinerary Engine & Realistic Market Sizing

I redesigned the product model around Bangladeshi group dynamics and built the interactive Tourmate web prototype. Users enter a single natural-language prompt in Bangla, Banglish, or English (e.g., "3-day Cox's Bazar trip, 4 people, 16,000 Tk budget"), and the system extracts destination, duration, headcount, and budget to generate a timed day-by-day schedule, verified hotel/transport/restaurant allocations, and human-customizable activity swaps.

03.4 // INSIGHT & OUTCOME SYNC → TAM / SAM / SOM FUNNEL

Grounded Unit Economics (BDT 150M TAM → BDT 2.25M Year-1 SOM)

By auditing and correcting initial AI currency and behavioral hallucinations, we established a realistic urban budget-travel market model: 30,000 target urban student/young-professional users × BDT 5,000 avg. spend = BDT 150M TAM, narrowing to a BDT 75M SAM (50% digital adoption) and a BDT 2.25M Year-1 SOM (~450 group bookings) with zero credit-risk exposure.
